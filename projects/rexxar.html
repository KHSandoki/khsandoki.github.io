Rexxar

A second self-built 3D printer, four years after the first — this time from published CAD and a parts list instead of a scrap bin. Under NT$7,000, running Klipper with a Voron toolhead.

Year
2022
Role
Sole builder
Stack
Klipper, Voron toolhead, aluminium extrusion, printed parts
Cost
Under NT$7,000 (~US$220)

From a blueprint, not a kit

Rexxar started from the CR-10's published F3D CAD. Rather than buying the kit, I used the model as a dimensioned reference: cut the aluminium extrusion to length myself, and sourced every motor, rail, driver and fastener individually.

The whole machine came in under NT$7,000 — a fraction of an assembled printer of the same size. Buying parts one at a time is slower and involves more decisions, but it is also the difference between owning a machine and understanding one. Every dimension in the frame was a number I had to look up and cut to.

Klipper instead of Marlin

The board runs Klipper, which moves motion planning off the microcontroller and onto a host computer. Three things follow from that, and they are why the choice was worth making:

  • Input shaping and pressure advance become practical, because there is real compute available to do the maths. Ringing and corner bulge stop being things you live with.
  • Configuration is a text file. Change a value, reload, test — no firmware rebuild, no reflash. On a machine you are still tuning, that turns an afternoon's iteration into a few minutes.
  • The 8-bit ceiling disappears. The microcontroller only steps motors; it is no longer the thing limiting how fast the machine can be driven.

The toolhead

The stock CR-10 hot end assembly was replaced with a Voron toolhead. The teal low-poly shroud in the photographs is a printed cover for it, carrying the part-cooling ducts and a light aimed at the bed — which sounds cosmetic until the first time you check a first layer without having to find a torch.

A printer printing its own parts is the pleasant part of this hobby: the shroud was designed, printed, found wanting, and reprinted several times, each iteration made by the machine it was for.

Four years separate Rexxar from Illuminator, and the instinct is identical — get the capability without paying for the packaging. What changed is that by 2022 the open-source ecosystem had done the hard parts for me. In 2018 I was cutting steel out of dead optical drives; in 2022 I was choosing between two excellent free firmwares.
